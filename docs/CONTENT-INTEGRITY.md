# Islamic Content Integrity and Source Policy

## Quran

- Use a recognized, traceable Quran text source and record the exact edition/version.
- Do not use generative AI to create, repair, complete, paraphrase, or translate Quranic Arabic.
- Preserve supplied Uthmani/IndoPak script, diacritics, ayah boundaries, and verse identifiers.
- Validate imported text against trusted reference data and keep checksums/version metadata.
- Any correction must be traceable to the source publisher and reviewed before release.

## Translation and Tafsir

- Identify translator, editor, edition, language, publisher, and source URL.
- Verify copyright and redistribution permissions before storing or distributing content.
- Keep translation/tafsir clearly distinguished from Arabic Quran text.
- Never present AI-generated explanation as a published tafsir.

## Hadith

- Store collection, book/chapter, narration identifier, Arabic text, translation, narrator chain metadata where available, grading, grader/source, and edition.
- Do not infer authenticity grades. Preserve disagreements and attribution where sources differ.
- Confirm licensing and permitted reuse for each collection/translation.

## Duas and Adhkar

- Store source references (Quran or hadith collection, identifier, and grading where applicable).
- Distinguish sourced prophetic supplications from general personal duas.
- Do not fabricate virtue claims, counts, or promised rewards.

## Audio

- Verify reciter identity, recording source, ownership, license, and permitted streaming/offline use.
- Do not scrape or redistribute recordings without permission.
- Preserve surah/ayah timestamps and metadata only when supplied by a reliable source.

## AI assistance

- Hifz/Tajweed features must be labeled as practice assistance.
- Do not claim certification, definitive correction, fatwa, or scholarly authority.
- Escalate uncertain religious questions to qualified scholars/sources.
